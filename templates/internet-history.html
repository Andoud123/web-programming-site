I- Introduction

The internet is a world-wide broadcasting capability and the reason why everyone is communicating now with each other by any device and sending information to any location in the world. It wasn’t invented in a year or a limited time in the past, but it took many years and decades to work on this successful tool and it was developed during the researches of specialists, governments, companies and universities. It started in 1950s, when the world was grappling with the Cold War, as a military project to a global web now.

II- Milestone and Timeline

1969 — Creation of ARPANET

ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency) was a concept declared by RAND, MIT and UCLA. In fall 1969, the first such node was installed in UCLA. By December 1969, there were four nodes on the infant network, which was named ARPANET. The four computers could transfer data on dedicated high-speed transmission lines. They could even be programmed remotely from the other nodes.

Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

1972 — Email becomes an important Internet Application

In 1972, the initial “hot” application, electronic mail, was introduced. In March Ray Tomlinson at BBN wrote the basic email message send and read software, motivated by the need of the ARPANET developers for an easy coordination mechanism. In July, Roberts expanded its utility by writing the first email utility program to list, selectively read, file, forward, and respond to messages. From there email took off as the largest network application for over a decade.

Source: Internet Society - Brief History of the Internet

1973–1983 — Development and adaptation of TCP/IP

DARPA-funded researchers expanded ARPANET and designed internet protocols. The original standard for communication was known as NCP, “Network Control Protocol,” but as time passed and the technique advanced, NCP was superceded by a higher-level, more sophisticated standard known as TCP/IP “Transmission Control Protocol”, allowing computers to transmit and receive data such as email, computer-to-computer file transfer and remote login by converting messages into packets at the source then reassembles them back into messages at the destination.

  • NSF - Birth of the Commercial Internet
  • Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

1986 — NSFNET expands the Internet

One of the most significant TCP/IP-based networks was NSFNET, launched in 1986 by NSF to connect academic researchers to a new system of supercomputer centers. As the first network available to every researcher, NSFNET became the de facto U.S. internet backbone, connecting around 2,000 computers in 1986 and expanding to more than 2 million by 1993. NSFNET laid the foundation for the internet's explosive worldwide growth in the 1990s.

Source: NSF - Birth of the Commercial Internet

1989 — The World Wide Web is proposed

Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist, invented the World Wide Web (WWW) in 1989, while working at CERN. The Web was originally conceived and developed to meet the demand for automated information-sharing between scientists in universities and institutes around the world. Tim wrote the first proposal for the World Wide Web in March 1989. The basic idea of the WWW was to merge the evolving technologies of computers, data networks and hypertext into a powerful and easy to use global information system.

Source: CERN - Short History of the Web

1991 — The World Wide Web becomes available

Berners-Lee wrote the first web page editor and developed the first web browser and web server, bringing his idea of the World Wide Web to life. It was the dawn of websites and web pages. This marked the beginning of web browsing and laid the foundation for websites and content sharing. This let people share and access documents and websites through a standardized system.

Source: GeeksForGeeks - History of the Internet

1995 — Commercial Internet expands

Commercial firms noted the popularity and effectiveness of the growing internet and began to build their own network infrastructure, eventually producing products that provided basic connectivity and internet services. During the 1990s, NSF helped shape the growth and operation of the modern internet, funding the development of the world's first freely available web browser “Mosaic“ and algorithms to reduce internet congestion. The rapid expansion of commercial internet services prompted NSF to shut down its dedicated infrastructure backbone in 1995 but continues to support many research projects to develop new networking tools, online educational resources and network-based applications.

Source: NSF - Birth of the Commercial Internet

III- 3 Key Technologies of the Internet

1. Packet Switching

Packet switching is a technology that divides messages into packets, each packet separately addressed. Then begin at some specified source node, and end at some other specified destination node. Each packet would wind its way through the network on an individual basis.

Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

2. TCP/IP

TCP/IP is the main communication protocol suite used by the Internet. It handles the addressing, seeing to it that packets are routed across multiple nodes and even across multiple networks with multiple standards.

Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

3. The World Wide Web

The World Wide Web (WWW) was invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989. It is a system that allows people to access and connect information through webpages, hyperlinks, and web browsers. The Web has made the Internet much easier for people to use and share information.

Source: CERN - Short History of the Web
