HISTORY OF THE INTERNET

Introduction | Milestone Timeline | Key Technologies | Reliable Sources

I- Introduction

The Internet is a worldwide communication system that allows people to communicate with each other using many different devices and to send information to locations around the world.

The Internet was not invented in one year. It took many years and decades of research and development by specialists, governments, companies, and universities.

Its development began in the 1950s during the Cold War. It started with military and research projects and eventually developed into the global Internet that we use today.

Sources:

  • Internet Society
  • GeeksforGeeks

II- Milestone Timeline

1969 — Creation of ARPANET

ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network) was an early computer network. In fall 1969, the first node was installed at UCLA. By December 1969, four computers were connected to the network.

These computers could transfer data using high-speed transmission lines and could also be programmed remotely from other nodes.

Source: Internet Society

1972 — Email Becomes an Important Internet Application

In 1972, electronic mail became an important application on ARPANET. Ray Tomlinson created software for sending and reading email messages.

Email quickly became one of the most important applications on the network.

Source: Internet Society

1973–1983 — Development and Adoption of TCP/IP

Researchers working with DARPA developed new Internet protocols. The original communication standard was called NCP (Network Control Protocol).

Later, TCP/IP (Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol) replaced NCP. TCP/IP allowed computers to communicate and exchange data between different networks.

Sources:

  • National Science Foundation
  • Internet Society

1986 — NSFNET Expands the Internet

NSFNET was launched in 1986 by the National Science Foundation. It connected academic researchers to supercomputer centers.

NSFNET became an important Internet backbone in the United States and helped the Internet grow during the 1990s.

Source: National Science Foundation

1989 — The World Wide Web Is Proposed

Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web in 1989 while working at CERN.

The Web was created to make it easier for scientists around the world to share information.

Source: CERN

1991 — The World Wide Web Becomes Available

Tim Berners-Lee created the first web page editor, web browser, and web server.

This was the beginning of web browsing and websites. It allowed people to access and share information through the Web.

Source: GeeksforGeeks

1995 — Commercial Internet Expands

Commercial companies started building their own Internet networks and providing Internet services.

The rapid growth of commercial Internet services led the NSF to shut down its dedicated backbone in 1995. The Internet continued to grow around the world.

Source: National Science Foundation

III- 3 Key Technologies of the Internet

1. Packet Switching

Packet switching divides information into small packets. Each packet can travel through the network to reach its destination. The packets are then put together again at the destination.

Source: Internet Society

2. TCP/IP

TCP/IP is the main communication protocol used by the Internet. It allows computers and different networks to communicate with each other and transfer data.

Source: Internet Society

3. World Wide Web

The World Wide Web was invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989. It allows people to access information through web pages, hyperlinks, and web browsers.

The Web made the Internet easier for people to use and share information.

Source: CERN
