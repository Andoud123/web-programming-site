HISTORY OF THE INTERNET

I- Introduction

The Internet is a worldwide communication system that allows people to communicate with each other using many different devices and to send information to locations around the world. It was not invented in a single year or during a limited period. Instead, it took many years and decades of research and development by specialists, governments, companies, and universities.

Its development began in the 1950s, during the Cold War, with important military and research projects. Over time, these technologies developed into the global Internet that we use today.

II- Milestone Timeline

1969 — Creation of ARPANET

ARPANET (Advanced Research Projects Agency Network) was an early computer network developed through research involving organizations including RAND, MIT, and UCLA. In fall 1969, the first node was installed at UCLA. By December 1969, four nodes were connected to the infant network named ARPANET. The computers could transfer data over dedicated high-speed transmission lines and could also be programmed remotely from other nodes.

Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

1972 — Email Becomes an Important Internet Application

In 1972, electronic mail became an important application on ARPANET. In March, Ray Tomlinson at BBN wrote basic software for sending and reading email messages. In July, Larry Roberts expanded email's usefulness by creating a program that could list, selectively read, file, forward, and respond to messages. Email quickly became one of the largest network applications for more than a decade.

Source: Internet Society - Brief History of the Internet

1973–1983 — Development and Adoption of TCP/IP

DARPA-funded researchers expanded ARPANET and developed new Internet protocols. The original communication standard was called NCP (Network Control Protocol). As networking technology advanced, TCP/IP (Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol) replaced NCP as the standard for communication between networks.

TCP/IP allowed computers to transmit and receive data such as email, computer-to-computer file transfers, and remote login. Data could be divided into packets at the source and reassembled at the destination.

Sources:
National Science Foundation - The Internet
Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

1986 — NSFNET Expands the Internet

NSFNET was launched in 1986 by the National Science Foundation (NSF) to connect academic researchers to a new system of supercomputer centers. It became an important U.S. Internet backbone. It connected around 2,000 computers in 1986 and expanded to more than 2 million by 1993. NSFNET helped create the foundation for the rapid growth of the Internet during the 1990s.

Source: National Science Foundation - The Internet

1989 — The World Wide Web Is Proposed

Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist working at CERN, proposed the World Wide Web in 1989. The Web was originally developed to meet the need for automated information sharing between scientists at universities and institutes around the world.

The basic idea was to combine computers, data networks, and hypertext into a powerful and easy-to-use global information system.

Source: CERN - A Short History of the Web

1991 — The World Wide Web Becomes Available

Berners-Lee created the first web page editor, web browser, and web server, bringing his World Wide Web idea to life. This marked the beginning of web browsing and laid the foundation for websites and online content sharing.

Source: GeeksforGeeks - History of the Internet

1995 — Commercial Internet Expands

Commercial companies recognized the popularity and usefulness of the growing Internet and began building their own network infrastructure and Internet services. During the 1990s, NSF supported Internet development, including the development of the Mosaic web browser and research into reducing Internet congestion.

The rapid growth of commercial Internet services led NSF to shut down its dedicated infrastructure backbone in 1995 while continuing to support research and educational Internet projects.

Source: National Science Foundation - The Internet

III- 3 Key Technologies of the Internet

1. Packet Switching

Packet switching is a technology that divides messages into smaller packets. Each packet is addressed separately and can travel through the network toward its destination. The packets are then combined again at the destination to reconstruct the original information.

Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

2. TCP/IP

TCP/IP is the main communication protocol suite used by the Internet. It provides the rules needed for addressing and transferring data, allowing packets to travel across multiple computers, networks, and different networking standards.

Source: Internet Society - Short History of the Internet

3. World Wide Web

The World Wide Web (WWW) was invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989. It is a system that allows people to access and connect information through web pages, hyperlinks, and web browsers. The Web made the Internet much easier for people to use and share information.

Source: CERN - A Short History of the Web
